1. UNIVERSAL DIVISIBILITY RULES:
   • Divisibility by 9:
     - GOLDEN RULE: Sum of ALL digits across the ENTIRE number must be a multiple of 9 (9, 18, 27, 36...).
     - EXAM TRAP: Never look at just front or back digits (e.g., in 81,235, seeing '81' is a trap; the sum is 19, so NOT divisible!).
     - EXAMPLE: 52,434 -> 5 + 2 + 4 + 3 + 4 = 18 -> Divisible by 9!

   • Divisibility by 11 (The Alternating Sum Trick):
     - GOLDEN RULE: |Sum of Odd-placed digits - Sum of Even-placed digits| = 0 or 11 or 22...
     - EXAMPLE: 65,714
       Group 1 (1st, 3rd, 5th digits): 6 + 7 + 4 = 17
       Group 2 (2nd, 4th digits): 5 + 1 = 6
       Difference = 17 - 6 = 11 -> Completely Divisible by 11!

   • Co-Prime Compound Divisor Rules (Super High-Yield in IBPS RRB PO):
     - Rule for 72: Split into co-primes 8 and 9 (HCF = 1). Must satisfy BOTH Divisibility by 8 and 9!
     - Rule for 88: Split into co-primes 8 and 11 (HCF = 1). Must satisfy BOTH Divisibility by 8 and 11!
     - TRAP ALERT: Never split 72 into 6 x 12 or 88 into 4 x 22 (they share common factors!).

★ COACH'S SPECIAL INTUITION GUIDE:
HOW TO DECIDE WHETHER TO PICK 18, 27, OR 36 WHEN SOLVING DIVISIBILITY BY 9 / 72?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question students often ask: "How do I guess whether to set the sum equal to 18, 27, or another multiple of 9?"

Here is the exact mathematical boundary and mental rule:
1. Every digit (x and y) can ONLY be a single digit from 0 to 9.
   Therefore, the minimum sum of two digits (x + y) is 0 + 0 = 0.
   The maximum possible sum of two digits (x + y) is 9 + 9 = 18.
   Mathematical Law: 0 <= (x + y) <= 18.

2. Let S_known be the sum of the known digits given in the question.
   Total sum = S_known + (x + y).
   Since 0 <= (x + y) <= 18:
   The total sum MUST lie strictly in the range:
   S_known <= Total Sum <= S_known + 18.

3. Step-by-Step Decision Protocol:
   • Look at Q2.1: Number is 65x42y. Divisible by 72 (needs sum of digits to be multiple of 9).
     Sum of known digits = 6 + 5 + 4 + 2 = 17.
     Possible range of total sum = 17 to (17 + 18) = 17 to 35.
     Which multiples of 9 fall between 17 and 35?
     Only two multiples exist: 18 and 27! (36 is > 35, so 36 is mathematically IMPOSSIBLE!).

   • Now test 18 vs 27 against the options:
     Case A: If Total Sum = 18:
             17 + (x + y) = 18  ==>  (x + y) = 18 - 17 = 1.
             Look at the options: [A] 8, [B] 10, [C] 12, [D] 9, [E] 11.
             Is 1 in the options? NO! So 18 is impossible.

     Case B: If Total Sum = 27:
             17 + (x + y) = 27  ==>  (x + y) = 27 - 17 = 10.
             Look at the options: Option [B] is 10! MATCH FOUND INSTANTLY!

   • Look at Q2.1B: Number is 41x32y.
     Sum of known digits = 4 + 1 + 3 + 2 = 10.
     Range of total sum = 10 to 28.
     Multiples of 9 in this range: 18 and 27.
     Case A: If Total Sum = 18:
             10 + (x + y) = 18  ==>  (x + y) = 8.
             Look at options: [A] 6, [B] 7, [C] 8, [D] 9, [E] 10.
             Option [C] is 8! MATCH FOUND! (No need to even test 27!).

GOLDEN SUMMARY FOR EXAM DAY:
1. Sum the known digits: S_known.
2. Next multiple of 9 after S_known gives the FIRST candidate.
3. If that candidate matches the options, MARK IT!
4. If that candidate is too small (e.g. 1), jump to the NEXT multiple of 9 (+9), which gives the answer!
5. Never go beyond S_known + 18 because digits cannot exceed 9.

2. UNIT DIGIT (LAST DIGIT) OF POWERS - THE UNIVERSAL CYCLICITY RULE:
   • Master Key: Divide the power by 4!
   • The remainder 'r' is the EFFECTIVE POWER:
     - Remainder 1 -> Base^1
     - Remainder 2 -> Base^2
     - Remainder 3 -> Base^3
     - Remainder 0 -> Base^4 (CRITICAL: Remainder 0 means full cycle of 4, NOT power 0!).
   • EXAM TRAP: The remainder is the EXPONENT/POWER, NOT the final answer!
     - 7^45: 45 / 4 = Remainder 1 -> Effective power = 7^1 = 7! (Do not write 1!).
   • Permanent Ending Digits (Never Change for any positive power):
     - 0 always ends in 0
     - 1 always ends in 1
     - 5 always ends in 5 (e.g., 5^100 ends in 5)
     - 6 always ends in 6 (e.g., 46^78 ends in 6)

3. TOTAL FACTORS & ODD/EVEN FACTORS FORMULA:
   • Prime-factorize N into: N = 2^a x p1^b x p2^c ...
   • Total Factors = (a + 1)(b + 1)(c + 1)...
   • ODD Factors ("Ignore the 2" Trick):
     - Powers of 2 make numbers even. To get ODD factors, completely delete 2^a!
     - Odd Factors = (b + 1)(c + 1)...
     - Example: 60 = 2^2 x 3^1 x 5^1 -> Odd factors = (1 + 1)(1 + 1) = 2 x 2 = 4 (Factors: 1, 3, 5, 15).
   • EVEN Factors:
     - Even Factors = Total Factors - Odd Factors = [a x (b + 1)(c + 1)...]

4. TRAILING ZEROS IN FACTORIALS (LEGENDRE'S FORMULA):
   • Trailing zeros are produced by 2 x 5 pairs. 2s are abundant, so count powers of 5:
     Number of Trailing Zeros = [N/5] + [N/25] + [N/125] + [N/625] ...
   • Example 1: 50! -> [50/5] + [50/25] = 10 + 2 = 12 zeros.
   • Example 2: 100! -> [100/5] + [100/25] = 20 + 4 = 24 zeros.
   • Example 3: 125! -> [125/5] + [125/25] + [125/125] = 25 + 5 + 1 = 31 zeros. (Don't forget 125!).

5. NEGATIVE REMAINDER SHORTCUT:
   • When dividing (M - 1)^n by M:
     Treat (M - 1) as -1 mod M.
     - If power n is ODD: (-1)^odd = -1 -> Final Remainder = M - 1.
     - If power n is EVEN: (-1)^even = +1 -> Final Remainder = 1.
   • Example: 14^99 ÷ 15 -> (-1)^99 = -1 -> Final Remainder = 15 - 1 = 14! (Done in 3 seconds).

6. ALGEBRAIC FACTOR THEOREM:
   • (a^n + b^n) is ALWAYS divisible by (a + b) whenever n is ODD!
     - Example: (43^29 + 17^29) is divisible by (43 + 17) = 60!
   • (a^n - b^n) is ALWAYS divisible by (a - b) for ALL natural powers n!
   • (a^n - b^n) is divisible by (a + b) whenever n is EVEN!

7. COMMON REMAINDER LCM RULE:
   • The smallest number which when divided by a, b, c leaves remainder r in each case:
     Number = LCM(a, b, c) + r
   • Example: Smallest number divided by 4, 6, 8 leaving remainder 3:
     LCM(4, 6, 8) = 24 -> Number = 24 + 3 = 27.

8. CONSECUTIVE NUMBERS & THE MIDDLE NUMBER PROPERTY:
   • For any odd count of consecutive numbers (3, 5, 7 numbers):
     Average = EXACT MIDDLE NUMBER!
   • Example: Sum of 5 consecutive even numbers = 140.
     Middle (3rd) number = 140 ÷ 5 = 28.
     Numbers: 24, 26, 28, 30, 32 -> Largest is 32!

9. HCF & LCM PRODUCT RELATION:
   • Fundamental Law: First Number x Second Number = HCF x LCM.
   • When numbers are in ratio a : b with HCF = H:
     Numbers are (H x a) and (H x b).
     LCM = H x a x b.

[Q1.1] Which of the following numbers is divisible by 9?
       [A] 43,721   [B] 56,412   [C] 72,342   [D] 81,235   [E] 65,311
       • Concept: Sum of all digits must be divisible by 9.
       • Mistake analyzed: Trapped by seeing '81' at the start of [D].
         In [D], sum is 8+1+2+3+5 = 19 (19 is not divisible by 9).
       • Correct Check: In [B], sum = 5+6+4+1+2 = 18. (18/9 = 2 cleanly).
       • Answer: [B] 56,412
       • Coach's Rule: Always sum the WHOLE number, never guess based on front or back digits.

[Q1.1B] Reinforcement Question: Which of the following numbers is divisible by 9?
        [A] 23,456   [B] 41,237   [C] 63,118   [D] 52,434   [E] 91,241
        • Solution: In [D], 5 + 2 + 4 + 3 + 4 = 18. Since 18 is a multiple of 9, it is divisible!
        • Answer: [D] 52,434

[Q1.2] Which of the following numbers is divisible by 11?
       [A] 48,271   [B] 65,714   [C] 53,429   [D] 39,282   [E] 71,425
       • Concept: Difference between sum of odd-placed digits and even-placed digits must be 0 or 11.
       • Solution for [B] (65,714):
         Odd positions (1st, 3rd, 5th digits): 6 + 7 + 4 = 17.
         Even positions (2nd, 4th digits): 5 + 1 = 6.
         Difference = 17 - 6 = 11. Completely divisible by 11!
       • Answer: [B] 65,714
       • Coach's Shortcut: Group digits in two alternating waves: (6, 7, 4) and (5, 1). Fast subtraction takes 5 seconds!

[Q1.3] What is the unit digit of 7^45?
       [A] 1   [B] 3   [C] 7   [D] 9   [E] 5
       • Concept: Cyclicity of 4 for powers. Divide power 45 by 4.
       • Step 1: 45 ÷ 4 = 11 with Remainder = 1.
       • Step 2: Remainder 1 means the unit digit is the same as 7^1 = 7.
       • Mistake analyzed: Remainder is 1, but do NOT select 1 as the answer! The remainder is the POWER.
       • Answer: [C] 7

[Q1.3B] Reinforcement Question: What is the unit digit of 3^13?
        [A] 1   [B] 3   [C] 7   [D] 9   [E] 5
        • Solution: 13 ÷ 4 gives remainder 1.
          Effective power = 3^1 = 3!
        • Answer: [B] 3

[Q1.4] How many total factors does 36 have?
       [A] 6   [B] 8   [C] 9   [D] 12   [E] 10
       • Concept: Prime factorize 36: 36 = 4 x 9 = 2^2 x 3^2.
       • Formula: Total factors = (a + 1)(b + 1) = (2 + 1)(2 + 1) = 3 x 3 = 9.
       • Answer: [C] 9
       • Fast Check: The factors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36 (exactly 9 factors).
       • Coach's Rule: Perfect squares always have an ODD number of total factors!

[Q1.5] How many trailing zeros are at the end of 50!?
       [A] 10   [B] 12   [C] 14   [D] 15   [E] 8
       • Concept: Legendre's Formula counting factors of 5.
       • Step 1: [50 / 5] = 10.
       • Step 2: [50 / 25] = 2.
       • Total Zeros = 10 + 2 = 12.
       • Answer: [B] 12
       • Coach's Rule: Keep dividing by 5 until the quotient is less than 5, then add quotients: 50/5 = 10; 10/5 = 2. Total = 10 + 2 = 12!

[Q2.1] If 65x42y is completely divisible by 72, find the value of (x + y).

       • Total sum = 17 + (x + y).
       • If sum = 18 -> (x + y) = 1 (Not in options).
       • If sum = 27 -> (x + y) = 27 - 17 = 10 (Option [B] matches!).
       • Answer: [B] 10

        [A] 6   [B] 7   [C] 8   [D] 9   [E] 10

[Q2.2] What is the remainder when 14^99 is divided by 15?

       • 3-Second Shortcut: 14 mod 15 is -1.
         (-1)^99 = -1 (since 99 is odd).
         Final positive remainder = 15 - 1 = 14!
       • Answer: [C] 14
       • Coach's Rule: (M - 1)^odd ÷ M ALWAYS leaves remainder (M - 1)!

[Q2.3] What is the unit digit of (23^45 x 46^78 x 77^43)?

       • Step 2 (46^78): Any power of 6 always ends in 6.

[Q2.4] The sum of 5 consecutive even numbers is 140. What is the largest of these numbers?

[Q2.5] Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 4, and their HCF is 5. What is their LCM?
       [A] 45   [B] 60   [C] 75   [D] 30   [E] 90
       • Formula: LCM = HCF x a x b = 5 x 3 x 4 = 60!

[Q2.6] How many ODD factors does the number 60 have?

[Q2.7] If a number is divisible by 88, it must be divisible by which co-prime pair?

[Q2.8] How many trailing zeros are at the end of 125!?

[Q2.9] The expression (43^29 + 17^29) is completely divisible by which number?

[Q2.10] What is the smallest number which when divided by 4, 6, and 8 leaves a remainder of 3 in each case?

[PYQ 11.1] (IBPS PO Prelims Model):
If the 7-digit number 54x32y4 is divisible by 72, find the value of (x + y).
• Method: Divisible by 8 and 9.
  For 8: last 3 digits '2y4' divisible by 8 -> 224/8=28 (y=2) or 264/8=33 (y=6).
  For 9: sum of digits = 5 + 4 + x + 3 + 2 + y + 4 = 18 + x + y.
  For 18 + (x + y) to be a multiple of 9:
  (x + y) can be 9 (sum = 27) or 18 (sum = 36).
  Options in exam: [A] 7, [B] 9, [C] 11, [D] 13 -> Answer: [B] 9!

[PYQ 11.2] (SBI PO / RRB PO Mains Model):
Find the remainder when 2^68 is divided by 33.
• Coach's Shortcut: Notice that 2^5 = 32 = (33 - 1) = -1 mod 33.
  Rewrite: 2^68 = 2^(5 x 13 + 3) = (2^5)^13 x 2^3.
  (32)^13 x 8 mod 33 = (-1)^13 x 8 = -1 x 8 = -8 mod 33.
  Positive Remainder = 33 - 8 = 25!
• Exam Hack: Convert large powers of 2 into powers close to the divisor (32 is 33 - 1).

[PYQ 11.3] (RRB PO Prelims Model):
How many trailing zeros are in the product (25! x 40!)?
• Trailing zeros in 25!: [25/5] + [25/25] = 5 + 1 = 6 zeros.
• Trailing zeros in 40!: [40/5] + [40/25] = 8 + 1 = 9 zeros.
• In multiplication, zeros ADD UP: 6 + 9 = 15 trailing zeros!
• Exam Trap: Do NOT multiply 6 x 9 = 54. Zeros always add!

[PYQ 11.4] (IBPS PO Prelims Model):
How many factors of 360 are multiples of 12?
• Prime factorization: 360 = 2^3 x 3^2 x 5^1.
• Since factors must be multiples of 12 (2^2 x 3^1), factor out 12:
  360 = 12 x (2^1 x 3^1 x 5^1).
• Total factors of the remaining part: (1 + 1)(1 + 1)(1 + 1) = 2 x 2 x 2 = 8 factors!
• Answer: 8 factors of 360 are divisible by 12.

[PYQ 11.5] (SBI PO Prelims Model):
Find the unit digit of (7^95 - 3^58).
• Unit digit of 7^95: 95 ÷ 4 leaves rem 3 -> 7^3 = 343 (ends in 3).
• Unit digit of 3^58: 58 ÷ 4 leaves rem 2 -> 3^2 = 9 (ends in 9).
• Subtraction: (ends in 3) - (ends in 9).
  Borrow 1: 13 - 9 = 4!
• Answer: Unit digit is 4!
• Exam Trap: Never do 3 - 9 = -6. Always borrow 10 just like normal subtraction!
