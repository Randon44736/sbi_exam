1. CORE PILLARS & ARCHITECTURE (ક્રિટિકલ રીઝનિંગના ૪ મુખ્ય સ્તંભો)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Critical Reasoning tests your ability to evaluate verbal statements objectively without bringing in personal feelings or external unstated information.

A) Statement & Assumption (વિધાન અને પૂર્વધારણા):
   - Definition: An assumption is something taken for granted *before* the speaker speaks.
   - THE NEGATION TEST (અવરોધક કસોટી — સર્વશ્રેષ્ઠ પદ્ધતિ):
     * Negate the assumption (add 'NOT').
     * If negating it invalidates or destroys the speaker's statement, then the assumption is 100% IMPLICIT (અંતર્ગત છે)!
     * Example: Statement: "Boil water during monsoon to prevent water-borne disease."
       Negation of Assumption: "Boiling water does NOT kill bacteria."
       Result: If boiling fails to kill bacteria, advising it makes no sense! Hence the assumption is IMPLICIT!

B) Statement & Course of Action (વિધાન અને કાર્યવાહી):
   - Definition: A logical step taken to resolve a problem or improve a situation.
   - The 3 Golden Rules:
     1. Feasibility (શક્યતા): Must be practically actionable.
     2. Direct Connection (સીધો સંબંધ): Must target the root problem, not symptoms.
     3. Proportionality (સંતુલિત): Must NOT be overly harsh or cause a greater crisis.
        (e.g., Dismissing 100 staff for a 5-minute delay is extreme and INVALID!).

C) Statement & Argument (મજબૂત વિરુદ્ધ નબળી દલીલ):
   - Strong Argument (મજબૂત દલીલ): Addresses the core issue directly, supported by constitutional rights, national welfare, or logical cause-effect.
   - Weak Argument (નબળી દલીલ): Emotional, ambiguous, based on personal opinion, or mere comparison ("Country X does it, so we should too").

D) Cause & Effect (કારણ અને પરિણામ):
   - Principle: Event A is the cause of Event B if Event A precedes Event B and has a direct causal link.
   - Check if both are independent effects of a common cause (e.g., both due to economic recession).

2. THE 6 GOLDEN ELIMINATION TRAPS (પરીક્ષામાં તાત્કાલિક વિકલ્પ કાઢવાના નિયમો)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Absolute Words Trap: Words like 'All', 'Only', 'Always', 'Never', 'Solely' (બધા જ, માત્ર, હંમેશાં, ક્યારેય નહીં) usually make assumptions/arguments INVALID!
2. Out-of-Scope Trap: Introducing external information not mentioned or logically deducible from the passage.
3. Punitive Extreme Action: Over-reacting actions in Course of Action questions are 100% rejected.
4. Mere Comparison: Comparing with another city or country without underlying context is a weak argument.
5. Simple Restatement: Merely paraphrasing the statement does NOT strengthen or weaken it.
6. The 45-Second Cap: In Mains, spend at most 45 seconds per Critical Reasoning question. If stuck between two close options, skip and return during the final review.

3. VERIFIED MAINS CASE STUDIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Case 1: Public Health Warning
Statement: "The civic authority has advised all citizens to wear masks in public transit during the winter surge of respiratory infections."
Assumption I: Masks significantly reduce transmission of airborne viruses. (IMPLICIT - Passes Negation Test).
Assumption II: Citizens will cooperate and wear masks. (IMPLICIT - An appeal assumes compliance).

Case 2: Traffic Congestion
Statement: "Peak-hour traffic congestion on the central expressway has doubled commuter travel times."
Course of Action I: Implement staggered office timings for government and private sectors. (FOLLOWS - Feasible & direct).
Course of Action II: Ban all private vehicles permanently from the city. (DOES NOT FOLLOW - Extreme and unfeasible).
